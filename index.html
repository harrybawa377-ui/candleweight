Explore the Data Behind Every 1-Minute Candle

Every 1-minute candle is more than a simple representation of price moving up or down. It is a compact record of market activity during a specific 60-second period. Within that single minute, the market creates an opening price, closing price, high, low, traded volume, price range, candle body, wicks, and several other measurable values.

Candle Strength is designed to make these details easier to observe, compare, and analyse. The focus is on the data behind each candle rather than predictions or predefined trading instructions. By examining individual measurements and their relationships, users can explore how market activity changes from one minute to the next.

Every Candle Has a Time and Sequence

A 1-minute candle belongs to a specific point in the trading session. Its timestamp identifies exactly when the recorded activity occurred, while its position in the sequence connects it with the candles before and after it.

This time-based structure makes every candle part of a larger dataset. A single minute can be studied independently, but consecutive minutes allow changes in price, volume, range, and other measurements to be tracked throughout the session.

OHLC: The Primary Candle Data

The four fundamental values of a candle are Open, High, Low, and Close, commonly referred to as OHLC.

The Open represents the first recorded price of the minute, while the Close represents the final recorded price. High and Low identify the highest and lowest prices reached during that period.

Together, these four values provide the basic numerical structure from which many other candle measurements can be calculated.

Measuring the Total Candle Range

The Candle range is calculated from the High and Low prices:

Range = High − Low

Range measures the complete price distance travelled within the minute. It can be small during relatively quiet periods and considerably larger during periods of increased price movement.

Range also provides information that the Open-to-Close change alone cannot show. A candle may finish close to its opening price while still recording a large High-to-Low movement.

Examining the Candle Body

The candle body represents the distance between the Open and Close.

Body Size = |Close − Open|

Body size can be examined as an absolute price value or as a percentage of the total candle range. This creates a numerical way to compare the internal structure of different candles.

Two candles with similar ranges can therefore have very different body sizes and consequently different structural characteristics.

Calculating Body-to-Range Ratio

The relationship between body size and total range provides another useful measurement.

Body-to-Range Ratio = Body Size ÷ Range

When the body occupies a large portion of the range, most of the candle's movement occurred between the opening and closing prices. When the ratio is small, a larger portion of the range exists outside the body.

This converts a visual characteristic into a measurable statistic that can be compared across hundreds or thousands of candles.

Reading the Upper Wick

The upper wick represents the distance between the upper part of the candle body and the High.

Its size indicates how far the price moved above the body during the minute. Recording upper-wick length allows users to compare how frequently candles reach higher price levels without focusing only on where the candle eventually closes.

Upper-wick measurements can also be examined as a percentage of the total range.

Reading the Lower Wick

The lower wick provides the corresponding measurement below the candle body. It represents the distance between the Low and the lower part of the body.

A candle can therefore be described using three structural components: body, upper wick, and lower wick.

Tracking these measurements across consecutive candles provides a numerical record of how candle structure changes over time.

Closing Position Within the Range

The closing price can also be measured according to its position between the High and Low.

One way to express this is:

Closing Position = (Close − Low) ÷ (High − Low)

This measurement places the Close within the candle's total range. Instead of simply recording whether the candle closed above or below its Open, it provides additional information about where the Close was located relative to the entire price movement of that minute.

Price Change and Percentage Change

Absolute price change and percentage change describe the movement between two prices.

For a candle:

Price Change = Close − Open

Percentage change can then be calculated relative to the opening price.

These values make candles with different price levels easier to compare because percentage movement provides a normalized measurement rather than relying only on the absolute price difference.

Volume Adds Another Dimension

Price structure is only one part of a 1-minute dataset. Volume records the quantity of trading activity associated with the candle.

A candle's range, body, and volume can be examined together. For example, users can compare candles with similar price ranges but different traded volumes, or observe how volume changes as candle ranges expand or contract.

This creates a broader numerical view of market activity.

Relative Volume and Historical Context

The volume of one candle becomes more informative when compared with other candles.

Relative volume can compare current activity with a selected historical reference, such as previous candles or the average volume for comparable periods.

This allows users to distinguish between an absolute volume value and its position relative to the surrounding dataset.

Comparing Candle Structure Across Minutes

The real depth of 1-minute data appears when individual candles are compared as a sequence.

Range may expand or contract. Body size may increase or decrease. Upper and lower wicks may change substantially. Volume may move from relatively low levels to considerably higher levels.

Instead of examining each candle independently, these changes can be recorded as a continuous series of measurements.

Identifying Changes in Market Activity

Market activity is not constant throughout an entire trading session. Different periods can contain different levels of price movement and trading volume.

A dataset containing every minute makes it possible to examine these changes numerically. Users can study when ranges become larger, when volume changes, and how candle structures vary between different parts of the session.

The objective is measurement rather than prediction.

From Individual Candles to a Dataset

One candle contains a limited number of observations. Hundreds or thousands of candles create a much larger dataset.

Each row can contain the timestamp, OHLC values, range, body, wick measurements, percentage change, volume, relative volume, closing position, and other calculated fields.

This structured approach makes it possible to sort, filter, compare, calculate averages, identify distributions, and study historical patterns in the data.

Current Candle Versus Previous Candle

Comparing the current candle with the immediately preceding candle can reveal how individual measurements have changed.

For example, users can calculate the difference in range, body size, volume, or percentage movement between two consecutive minutes.

These changes can be represented numerically rather than relying only on the visual appearance of a chart.

Current Data Versus Historical Data

A current candle can also be compared with historical candles from the same time period.

For example, the current 1-minute volume or range can be compared with the corresponding measurements from previous trading sessions. Historical averages, medians, minimums, maximums, and distributions can provide additional context around the current observation.

This transforms isolated market data into a comparative dataset.

Understanding Candle Strength as a Measurement

The term Candle Strength can represent a collection of measurable candle characteristics rather than a simple up-or-down classification.

Depending on the methodology, measurements such as range, body size, body-to-range ratio, volume, relative volume, price change, wick proportions, and closing position can be analysed together.

The underlying values remain important because they show exactly what contributes to any calculated measurement.

OHLC: The Four Primary Data Points

Open, High, Low and Close form the foundation of every candle. The Open records the first traded price of the period, while the Close records the final traded price. High represents the highest traded price and Low represents the lowest traded price during that minute. These four values define the complete price boundaries of the candle. From them, several additional measurements can be calculated, including absolute price change, percentage change and total range. Keeping these values visible makes it possible to understand exactly how each candle was formed.

Total Range: High Minus Low

Total candle range is calculated as High minus Low. It measures the complete distance travelled between the highest and lowest traded prices during the minute. Range is independent of whether the candle closes above or below its Open. A candle can therefore have a large range even when its Open and Close are relatively close together. Comparing the current range with previous candle ranges provides a numerical way to observe whether the size of price movement is expanding or contracting.

Body Size and Body Percentage

Candle body size is calculated from the difference between Close and Open. The absolute body size shows the magnitude of the movement between the beginning and end of the candle, while the body-to-range ratio shows how large the body is relative to the candle's complete range. For example, a body occupying most of the total range represents a different candle structure from a body occupying only a small percentage of the range. These measurements allow candle structure to be compared numerically rather than judged only from its appearance.

Upper Wick and Lower Wick

The distance between the High and the upper edge of the body forms the upper wick, while the distance between the Low and the lower edge of the body forms the lower wick. Measuring each wick separately provides information about how much price movement occurred beyond the Open-Close body. Wick-to-range percentages can also be calculated to compare the relative size of the upper and lower portions across different candles.

Closing Position Within the Range

The Close can be measured against the candle's High-Low range to determine where the candle finished within its total price movement. A close near the High represents a different closing position from a close near the Low, even when two candles have similar ranges. This measurement can be expressed as a percentage of the total range, providing a consistent numerical value that can be compared across consecutive candles.

Price Change and Percentage Change

The difference between the current Close and the previous reference price provides the absolute price change, while percentage change expresses that movement relative to the reference price. These values allow candles from different price levels to be compared more consistently. A movement of the same number of points can represent very different percentages at different price levels, which is why both absolute and percentage measurements can be useful when examining candle data.

Volume and Relative Volume

Volume records the quantity traded during the candle's one-minute period. Looking at the current volume alone provides one measurement, while comparing it with previous candles creates additional context. Relative volume can show how the current candle's activity compares with a selected historical or recent average. Combining volume with range and body measurements allows users to examine whether changes in price movement occurred alongside changes in trading activity.

Comparing One-Minute Candles

The data becomes more informative when individual candles are compared with their neighbouring candles. Current range can be compared with previous ranges, current volume with previous volume, and current body percentage with earlier body percentages. Changes in these measurements can be tracked sequentially, creating a numerical timeline of candle characteristics. Instead of looking at isolated candles, users can examine how each measurement changes from minute to minute.

Go Deeper Into Every 1-Minute Candle

A 1-minute candle is not just a point on a chart. It is a time-stamped snapshot of continuously changing market data. Going deeper means examining how the information is distributed across the minute and how different measurements relate to one another. Instead of treating every candle as an isolated shape, the data can be organized into a detailed record containing price levels, traded quantity, transaction activity and calculated statistics. This deeper view helps turn a sequence of candles into a structured dataset that can be examined from multiple angles.

Time and Sequence Matter

Every candle has a precise position within the trading session. The same candle characteristics can have different context depending on when they occur. Recording the exact minute allows observations to be grouped by time and compared across sessions. Minute-by-minute data can therefore be studied as a sequence rather than as unrelated observations. This creates a timeline in which changes in individual measurements can be tracked throughout the session.

Price Levels Within the Minute

The High and Low establish the boundaries reached during the minute, but the sequence of price levels between those boundaries is also part of the underlying activity. Tick-by-tick observations can show how frequently prices changed and how long different price levels remained active. While an OHLC candle compresses this information into four values, underlying tick data provides a much deeper layer for examining the formation of the candle.

Tick Activity and Trade Count

Two candles with similar OHLC values can be created through very different trading activity. One may contain a relatively small number of trades, while another may contain substantially more transactions. Trade count and tick frequency can therefore provide additional measurements alongside price and volume. These values help describe how actively the market was updating during a particular minute without converting the information into a trading recommendation.

Traded Quantity Distribution

Total volume gives the quantity traded during a minute, but the distribution of that quantity can also be examined when detailed trade data is available. Large individual trades and smaller repeated trades contribute differently to the total. Looking at traded quantity at a finer level can therefore provide additional detail about how the minute's total volume was formed.

Bid and Ask Depth

Market depth provides another layer of information beyond executed trades. Bid prices and quantities represent resting demand at different price levels, while ask prices and quantities represent resting supply. Tracking multiple depth levels can show how displayed quantities change during the minute. Because orders can be added, modified or removed, depth data is dynamic and can complement executed-price and volume information.

Spread and Depth Changes

The difference between the best available ask and best available bid forms the bid-ask spread. Monitoring this spread through time provides a separate measurement of the displayed market. Changes in the spread can be recorded alongside changes in top-level quantities and deeper order-book levels. This creates a more detailed dataset around the candle than price information alone can provide.

Data Frequency Changes

Market data does not always arrive at a uniform rate. Some minutes may contain many updates while others contain fewer observable changes. Measuring the number of price, trade or depth updates can provide information about the frequency of market-data activity. This is different from measuring price movement or total volume and can add another dimension to the study of individual minutes.

Turning Minutes Into a Dataset

Once each minute contains multiple fields—timestamp, OHLC, volume, trade activity, spread and depth measurements—the result is more than a conventional candlestick chart. It becomes a structured dataset that can be filtered, compared and analysed at different levels of detail. Users can examine individual minutes, sequences of minutes, selected time periods or historical sessions. The deeper the dataset, the more dimensions are available for understanding what actually occurred. Candle Strength is about making those layers of 1-minute market data accessible, measurable and easy to explore.
